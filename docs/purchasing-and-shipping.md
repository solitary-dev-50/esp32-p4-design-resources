# Purchasing and Shipping Notes

This page is a practical checklist for people who can find ESP32-P4 design information but cannot easily buy the chip locally.

## Buying the correct device

For a new design, look for the current `X` variants:

- `ESP32-P4NRW16X`
- `ESP32-P4NRW32X`

Avoid ordering only from a listing titled "ESP32-P4". Ask for:

- the complete ordering code;
- silicon revision;
- date/lot code where available;
- package type;
- quantity and packaging method;
- a clear photograph of the top marking if the source is not an authorized distributor.

## Small-quantity sourcing

For an early prototype:

1. buy a small number of chips first;
2. keep the seller invoice and listing screenshot;
3. inspect the package marking on arrival;
4. store QFN devices in suitable ESD and moisture-protective packaging;
5. verify one board before buying a larger quantity.

## International forwarding

A community member in another country may be able to purchase and forward chips at cost, but both sides should agree in writing on:

- chip price and quantity;
- actual postage;
- payment fees;
- customs declarations and taxes;
- tracking method;
- responsibility for loss or customs rejection;
- whether returns are possible;
- whether the chips have been electrically tested.

Do not describe an informal forwarding arrangement as an authorized distribution service.

## Customs and carrier restrictions

Integrated circuits are usually straightforward to ship, but rules differ by origin, destination, value, carrier, and current export-control requirements. The sender and recipient should check the current rules before payment and shipment.

This repository does not provide legal, customs, tax, or export-control advice.

## Safer community-help wording

A transparent offer can be phrased like this:

> ESP32-P4 chips are easier to obtain where I live. If you cannot find the exact current part locally, I may be able to buy a small quantity and forward it at the actual chip cost plus postage. We would confirm the exact ordering code and shipping/customs details first.

This avoids implying stock, warranty, or authorized-distributor status.
